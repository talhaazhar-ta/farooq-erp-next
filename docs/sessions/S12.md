## Goal
Server-side `PurchasesService` in `apps/api/src/purchases/`:
- **create** a purchase and **edit** a recorded one, each in one transaction;
- stock, the supplier's balance, the payment made with the purchase and the **average cost** written together;
- the legacy messages verbatim;
- the four M3 decisions enforced;
- a ledger-bridge proof like S7's.

No UI (S13 / S14).

## User decisions (2026-09-25 — enforce, test, don't reopen)
1. **Average cost is maintained here.** After every save, recompute `stock_levels.avg_cost_p` / `last_cost_p` for every product × warehouse the purchase touches **or used to touch** (dropped lines).
   - Use S11's `weightedAverage` with the settings basis (`LANDED` in the real data).
   - `null` (nothing received) keeps the old average. That is the legacy rule for a dropped product (`17-profit.js` wrapper).
   - `last_cost_p` = the line's unit on the chosen basis.
   - Store `goods_unit_cost_p` / `charge_share_p` / `landed_unit_cost_p` on each line (`allocateCharges`), and **keep `operational_share_p`** as it is. It belongs to M6's landed costs; the legacy 26 wrapper folds it back into `landed_unit_cost_p`.
   - **Never nudge** the average from the previous figure: always recompute from every purchase line. `test-stock-value.mjs` M5: a later typed receipt cost does not override a purchase-kept average.
2. **Permissions:**
   - create = `PURCHASE_CREATE` (OWNER, MANAGER);
   - edit = `PURCHASE_CREATE` **or** `TRANSACTION_CORRECT` (legacy `canEdit`, `02-services.js:1019-1022`);
   - a payment made with the purchase also needs `PAYMENT_PAYOUT` (403, and the whole save is refused);
   - INVENTORY and SALES: none.
3. **Legacy bugs fixed:**
   - `paid` below 0 or above the total is refused on a **new** purchase, too. Reuse the legacy edit wording for "more than the total".
   - A line discount above the line amount is refused, with a new message in the legacy style.
4. No drafts, no cancel, no delete, no separate Change supplier, no `receiveMore`.

## Rules (each gets a test)
- **Create.** One transaction:
  - number `PUR-<business year>-<6>` via `nextNumber` (the prefix comes from the settings, default `PUR`);
  - lines with stable ids and snapshots;
  - status ORDERED / PARTIALLY_RECEIVED / RECEIVED from Σ received vs Σ ordered;
  - `PURCHASE_IN` per product × warehouse with received > 0, `unit_cost_p` = unit price as in the legacy, `source_type` PURCHASE, dated the purchase date;
  - **Received = 0 posts the bill only**, with no stock;
  - the average cost (decision 1);
  - one PURCHASE journal entry (`purchaseLines`);
  - audit `Purchase recorded`.
- **Payment with the purchase.** If `paid > 0`, write one PV voucher through **one** payout core shared with `PaymentsService.pay`. Extract `writePayout(tx, …)` the way S7 extracted `writeReceipt`, and leave S3's tests unchanged. The voucher gets:
  - the note `Paid with purchase <number>`;
  - reference = the supplier invoice number (legacy);
  - the purchase date;
  - one allocation to this purchase, capped by S3's rules.
- **Edit.** Stale revision refused (the invoice wording pattern). Everything in `editErrors`, verbatim:
  - a returned or landed line cannot be removed or re-producted;
  - received cannot go below returned;
  - supplier locked by a payment or a return;
  - paid cannot be lowered;
  - paid above the total refused;
  - **net stock guard** per product × warehouse (honours `allowNegativeStock`).

  What an edit does:
  - **Stock moves by the net difference** per product × warehouse. The legacy did a full reverse and re-add; the net is the same. `PURCHASE_IN` / `PURCHASE_REVERSAL_OUT`, `ref_type` `PURCHASE_EDIT`. Pick one dating rule and justify it: the legacy dates the reversal on the original date and the re-add on the new date.
  - The one journal entry is rewritten in place (amount and date).
  - Raising paid → one extra voucher for the difference.
  - Line ids stay stable; dropped lines are deleted, unless they carry returns or landed costs, which is refused.
  - The average is recomputed (decision 1).
  - Audit `Purchase edited`, with before and after.
- **Idempotency** through `request_keys` (kind `PURCHASE`). **Concurrency:** lock order purchase → supplier → stock levels (sorted). Two edits of one revision → one wins; two creates racing → consecutive numbers.
- **Reads:** `GET /purchases/:id` returns header, lines, payments and allocations, the stock effect, and `actions {edit}` with a reason. Cost fields are shown only with `PROFIT_VIEW`. The supplier pickers (products mapped to a supplier first is **not** ported — `supplierProducts` is deferred; say so) and the last purchase rate per product.
- **Payment reverse** refreshes nothing stored: the payment status is derived. Prove a reversed PV lowers `paid` on `GET /purchases/:id`.
